2. In short

Stackhouse stores everything on your device: the buildings, their floors and apartments, and the people you note down in them. There is no server that the app transfers this data to, and it does not create an automatic backup in any cloud. It is set up so that Android and iCloud backups leave this data out. We have no access to it and can neither view nor restore it.

Without the “Premium” one-time purchase, the app uses Google AdMob. In doing so, technical data and device identifiers may be transmitted to Google and its advertising partners; which processing takes place depends in particular on your consent decision. With “Premium”, no more ads are loaded, and the app no longer asks for consent either.

On iOS the operating system additionally asks whether the app may use your advertising identifier (App Tracking Transparency). Without that permission the identifier is not transmitted.

What you note down usually concerns other people. How you handle that is up to you; section 4 explains what that means.

3. Data that stays on your device

The app stores everything you enter locally: your buildings with their floors and apartments, and the people you note down in them. The app’s settings are stored as well.

This data is not transmitted to us or to third parties. We have no access to it and can neither view nor restore it. The app is configured so that its data is excluded from automatic Android backup (allowBackup="false" together with exclusion rules, including for transfer to a new device); on iOS the folder holding the data is excluded from iCloud backup. Whatever the operating system and device manufacturer back up beyond that is outside our influence. For moving to a new device, rely on the export function.

You can export the data at any time to a file via “Back up data”; where that file goes is entirely your decision, made in your device’s save dialog. On both systems this file is the route Stackhouse provides for taking your data to a new device. You delete a building in the settings under “Buildings,” together with everything attached to it; “Delete all my data” removes all of it. Uninstalling the app deletes it as well.

4. Data about other people

Anyone who notes down a neighbor with a name and a phone number is processing their personal data. The controller for that processing is not the provider of the app, but the person carrying it out, that is, you.

If you use Stackhouse privately, for your own residential building for example, the exemption for purely personal or household activity generally applies (Art. 2(2)(c) GDPR): the obligations of the regulation do not apply to it.

If you use Stackhouse professionally (as a property manager, building maintenance service or landlord), that exemption does not apply. You are then responsible for the data you note down yourself: for the legal basis, informing the people concerned, deletion periods and requests for access. The app supports you in this by transmitting nothing, keeping everything local and making every entry deletable.

5. Advertising (Google AdMob) and consent

As long as “Premium” has not been purchased, the app embeds advertising from Google AdMob. The provider is Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland.

In this context Google may process, in particular, the IP address and an approximate location derived from it, device and advertising identifiers, information about app launches and interactions with the app and with ads, and diagnostic and performance data. Which identifiers are available and what they may be used for depends on your consent and on your system settings. Google states the purposes as advertising, analytics and fraud prevention.

What you have entered in the app is not transmitted to Google or to the advertising partners. The ads have no access to it and are not based on it.

Advertising partners are involved alongside Google. If you consent, third-party advertising companies may also process this data and store or read information on your device, for personalized advertising, measurement of ad performance and audience research, for example. Exactly who they are is listed in the consent dialog under “List of partners”; the list is always current there, because it can change. Under “Manage options” in the same dialog you can consent to or object to individual purposes and providers separately.

Before the first ad request, the app obtains your decision via Google’s consent platform (UMP); until that decision exists, no ad is requested. Which ads and which data processing are permitted depends on your decision: without the consent required for storing or reading information on your device, only limited ads may be delivered in some cases. The app remains fully usable either way.

Insofar as consent is required for the use of AdMob, the storing or reading of information on your device is based on § 25(1) TDDDG and the associated processing of personal data on Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR. Further processing by Google and the advertising partners follows their own statements on purposes and legal bases; individual providers rely on a legitimate interest, which you can object to in the dialog under “Manage options.”

If you decline, limited ads may be requested. Your IP address is transmitted to Google for those too, because otherwise an ad cannot be delivered. The legal basis in that respect is § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, that is, our interest in financing the app through advertising as long as “Premium” has not been purchased.

Processing may also take place outside the European Economic Area, in particular in the USA, both by Google and by the advertising partners. For transfers to Google LLC, Google relies on the European Commission’s adequacy decision on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (Art. 45 GDPR), under which Google LLC is certified, and otherwise on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses (Art. 46 GDPR). Further information, including how to view the clauses, at https://policies.google.com/privacy/frameworks. Which safeguards the individual advertising partners rely on is stated in their entries in the partner list of the consent dialog.

Retention: the data processed via AdMob is stored neither by us nor by the app on your device. How long Google and the advertising partners keep it depends on the type of data and the purpose and is stated in their privacy notices; Google states, for example, that it anonymizes advertising log data after fixed periods. Details are in the Google notices linked below and in the partners’ entries in the consent dialog.

Additionally on iOS: App Tracking Transparency

On iOS, Apple asks via a system dialog whether Stackhouse may use your advertising identifier (IDFA) in order to record your activity across other companies’ apps and websites. Apple’s dialog shows a short explanation from the app: “Allows the advertising identifier to be used for ads that suit you and to measure them. This is how the free version pays for itself.” Outside Europe, an additional explanation page from Google may appear beforehand; in Europe the consent dialog takes its place. iOS shows the dialog once per installation as a rule, after the GDPR consent, provided your system settings permit the request. The two decisions are independent of one another.

Without “Allow,” the app receives no access to the advertising identifier (IDFA), and Google may not track you across other companies’ apps and websites. Which advertising you then see, and which other identifiers Google may use, depends on your selection in the consent dialog. The app remains fully usable; you suffer no disadvantage.

For measuring the success of ads, Google may use Apple’s SKAdNetwork. No identifier of your device is transmitted in that process.

With “Premium”, no advertising is loaded; the dialog then does not appear.

You can change your decision at any time: in the settings under “Privacy options.” You change the tracking permission on iOS in the iOS settings under Privacy & Security → Tracking, at the switch for Stackhouse.

Further information: https://policies.google.com/privacy and https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

  • “Business”: unlocks further buildings permanently.

Both are handled by the store you obtained the app from. Depending on the source, that is:

  • Apple App Store: handled by Apple Distribution International Ltd., Cork, Ireland. Privacy notice: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/app-store/

The same applies in all cases: payment and billing data is processed by the respective store operator; we receive no complete payment data such as card or bank details. From the store, the app receives only the information needed to establish and manage the purchase status, and stores the result locally. The purchase status serves solely to unlock what you have purchased. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract).

Both purchases apply permanently. If the purchase of “Business” is refunded, your data stays untouched: the additional buildings are then merely locked, and they are accessible again as soon as “Business” is purchased again.

A purchase applies only in the store where it was made. Anyone who later obtains the app from a different source has no purchase there; restoring always runs through the store where the purchase was made.

7. No additional analytics or tracking services

We use no analytics, tracking or crash-reporting services of our own. There are no user accounts, no registration and no profiling by us. The processing that takes place in the context of Google AdMob (which includes analytics purposes of Google’s) is described in section 5.

9. Contacting us

If you write to us by email, we process your address and the content of the message in order to answer your inquiry. For questions about a purchase this happens on the basis of Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, otherwise on the basis of Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our interest in answering your inquiry). The mailbox is provided by Google (Gmail); Google processes the messages in accordance with its own privacy notice. We delete the correspondence as soon as the inquiry is settled and no retention obligation applies.

10. Your rights

Subject to the statutory requirements, you have in particular the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing and data portability. Insofar as we process personal data on the basis of Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, you have the right to object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to that processing (Art. 21 GDPR). You object to processing by Google and the advertising partners on the basis of legitimate interests in the consent dialog under “Manage options.” You can withdraw consent once given at any time with effect for the future: for the advertising consent via “Privacy options” in the settings, for the tracking permission on iOS via the iOS settings under Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Provision of data: you do not have to give us any personal data in order to use the app; there is no account and no registration. Consent to advertising and, on iOS, to tracking is voluntary. If you decline, the app remains fully usable. Only for purchasing “Premium” or “Business” do you have to provide the respective store with the data needed to process it; without it the purchase cannot be carried out. Contacting us by email is likewise voluntary.

To exercise your rights you can contact us at any time (contact details above). Your data lies exclusively on your device; we have no access to it, and the advertising data processed by Google is not held by us. Insofar as Google or an advertising partner is independently responsible for a processing operation, you can additionally assert your rights directly with them. You will find the providers and their contact routes in the consent dialog under “List of partners.”

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, in particular with the authority responsible for us, the State Commissioner for Data Protection of Lower Saxony, or with the supervisory authority at your place of residence, your place of work or the place of the alleged infringement.

11. Changes

If the app’s data processing changes, this policy will be adjusted. The version published at the time is the one that applies.
